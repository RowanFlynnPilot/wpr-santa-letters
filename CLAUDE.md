# Letters to Santa — project guide

Wausau Pilot & Review's holiday mailbag, a revival of the feature WPR ran in 2022 and 2024 (one article of letters, first name and age, published Dec. 23, with prizes drawn at random). Built Nov. 7, 2026 on the Buck Board's pattern (`wpr-buck-board`): Supabase + React/Vite on GitHub Pages + a WordPress iframe, Turnstile on the form, staff sign-in by emailed code. There is no voting.

**2026 calendar** (America/Chicago, stored in `seasons`): letters open Nov. 27 (the day after Thanksgiving) · close at the end of Dec. 18 · staff finish the mail, review and run the drawing Dec. 19–22 · delivered Dec. 23 at 6 a.m.: the article runs and winners are public. Shereen can move any of these in `supabase/seasons/2026.sql` before it's run, or with an `update` after.

**How letters arrive** (as in past years): the form (`#/write`, a parent or guardian fills it in for the child), email to editor@, and the mail (Santa, care of WPR, PO Box 117, Wausau, WI 54402; `web/src/contact.ts`). Staff type in emailed and mailed letters at `#/admin` > Add a letter.

**A child who writes by hand has paper to write on** (`#/paper`, `Paper.tsx`; built Nov. 21). It is the reply's stationery, blank: "My name is ____ and I am __ years old. I live in ____" (what the mailbag publishes, and nothing more), seven ruled rows, a box to draw in, a stamp to draw, and at the foot, under a dashed fold line, a part for a grown-up: a name and an email to write in, the mailing address with the date mail has to arrive by, the page's address for sending a photo instead, and what sending the letter agrees to, in the form's words. The email is the point: a mailed letter with no way to reach the family can't be told it's up, sent Santa's reply or drawn for a prize (invariant 10). **Staff fold the grown-up's part under before photographing a mailed letter**, as the sheet tells a parent to before photographing it for the form, so the photo in the mailbag never shows it. The mailbag (Other ways to send a letter) and the form (under the photo box) link to it in a new tab: like Santa's reply it is opened on its own page, not inside WordPress, so it prints as one sheet (it shares the reply's frame, night and print rules; everything a child writes on is a border, which prints whether or not backgrounds do). It reads only the season, and never depends on it (as invariant 26 has it for the pick): without the season it shows without the arrive-by date; once letters close it says so instead of offering paper. It names no sponsor: a new place for one is Shereen's and Chris's to decide. The consent wording on it mirrors the form's and RULES.md; it changes with them. Plausible counts `Letter Paper Printed`, with nothing else said.

**The launch kit** (`docs/social`, `web/tests/social.mjs`; `scripts/browser-tests.sh social`): three pictures to post (letters open, a week left, delivery day), each for a feed (1080 by 1350) and as a story (1080 by 1920), on the night with the envelope, and `posts.md` with the words for each. All of it is made from `docs/social/kit.json` (the presenting sponsors' names in the order they signed on, the three dates, the page's address), so a sponsor signing on is a change to that file and one run; every picture and every post names the presenting sponsors, as the sheet promises. A sponsor's name is never split across lines, and the script refuses a picture whose words don't fit rather than making one that runs under the horizon.

**Selling it:** `docs/sponsor-sheet/` is the one-page pitch (three levels, WPR's four media-kit audience figures and no others). The prices were set by Shereen and Chris on Nov. 11, 2026, and change only on their word: a presenting sponsor (four at most, decided Nov. 15) pays $500 and gives a prize for the drawing; a supporting sponsor pays $300 and is named in the row under the envelope and at the foot; a prize donor gives a prize worth $75 to $100 and pays nothing. Beyond what the site does, a presenting sponsor gets a banner ad rotating on WPR's homepage and on the Letters to Santa page, Nov. 23 through Jan. 1, 970 by 500 pixels (on the Letters to Santa page it is this app's own place above the letters, below; on the homepage it is WPR's ad space in WordPress) and is named in every post promoting the mailbag; its pictures are real screenshots in preview mode (`scripts/browser-tests.sh shots`), which shows all four presenting places: the sponsors signed on, "Your business here" in the next, a blank stamp for each one still open, and a supporting sponsor's place reading "Your business here" in the row under the envelope. Rebuild it when what a sponsor gets changes. Chris (Christine) Weber's address is weber.chris@wausaupilotandreview.com (`web/src/sales.ts`); the sheet carries her own line, 715-218-5079.

**Sponsors:** up to four presenting sponsors (the database refuses a fifth: migration 0011) and any number of supporting sponsors: named, by logo or name and linked, in a "With support from" row right under the envelope and above the letters (`Supporters` in `Mailbag.tsx`, placement `supporters`), and thanked again at the foot with everyone else. At the foot alone they sat below every letter in the mailbag. A presenting sponsor's logo is a stamp on the mailbag's envelope, or until the logo arrives its name in type, sized so no word ever splits (`widestWord` in `looks.ts`). One sponsor is the single large stamp with "Presented by …" under it, as at launch; two to four sit side by side (`Stamps` in `Stamp.tsx`: two across in the envelope's corner and on the strip, a row above the title on a phone) with one line naming them all, in the order they signed on. They share the mailbag, the strip and the thank-you page; where there is room for one stamp (a letter saved as a picture, Santa's reply) they take turns by letter (`turnFor` in `looks.ts`, `presentingFor` in `sales.ts`), so a letter keeps its stamp and each sponsor gets an even share, and a sponsor signing on mid-season reshuffles the turns. The newsletter section, the Dec. 23 article and the reply's foot name them all. Prizes are their own rows, one per prize to win. **A prize donor is a level of its own** (`donor`, migration 0012): a prize has to hang on a sponsor's row, and entered as `supporting` a business that only gives a prize would sit in the row under the envelope that a supporting sponsor pays $300 for. A donor is named with its prize, with its winner on delivery day, and last among "Thanks to our sponsors" at the foot (presenting, then supporting, then donors, each in the order they signed on); never on a stamp or in that row.

**The presenting sponsors' banner ads are in the mailbag** (decided Nov. 20, when Chris sent the first two: until then the ad was to be WordPress's alone). One place, above the letters (`SponsorAd.tsx`; under the three steps before there are any letters), shows one sponsor's own artwork a visit, under a small "Presenting sponsor" label, linked where the sponsor asked, marked `sponsored` and counted as a `Sponsor Click` with the `banner` placement. Which one is drawn as the page loads (`pickAd` in `ads.ts`), so each sponsor gets an even share of visits and the ad doesn't change under a reader; `?ad=2` on the app's address shows the second in the list, for checking one or showing it to its sponsor. **The ads are files, not rows:** the artwork sits in `web/public/ads` (970 by 500, and the same at 1940 wide where the sponsor's file had it to give) and `ads.json` beside it lists each one (the sponsor's name as its row has it, the link, the file, what it says for a reader who can't see it, and `through`, its last day in Wausau: Jan. 1). A new ad is a picture and a few lines in a pull request, with nothing to run in production (README, A presenting sponsor's banner ad). The list is asked for with the letters and the picture's room is held by its width and height, so nothing moves to make room; and like the editor's pick the page never depends on it (invariant 26): a list that's missing or mistyped, an entry with a link that isn't `https`, or an ad past its last day is simply no ad. A sponsor's files are re-encoded before they're committed, which drops whatever a phone wrote into them. The homepage's rotation is still WordPress's, with the same files.

**A logo fills its stamp, whatever the file.** It's scaled to the largest that fits (a small file saved off a Facebook page is scaled up), and it can be PNG, JPEG, WebP or SVG under 1 MB (the `sponsor-logos` bucket's limits). A square or stacked logo suits an upright stamp. A wordmark (twice as wide as it's tall, or wider) is a sliver on the small stamps several sponsors share, so there its name is set in type under it (`WORDMARK` in `Stamp.tsx`); on the single large stamp, with "Presented by" and the name right under it, the logo stands alone. What to ask a new sponsor for is in the README (Sponsor sheet, When a sponsor signs on). **A logo on white shows no white box** (Nov. 24, when the first real logos went up: most arrive on a white background, and a JPEG has no other kind): in the row under the envelope, on a prize's tag and at the foot, a logo is multiplied with what's behind it (`mix-blend-mode`), so its white takes the cream's colour and its own colours are all but unchanged. A stamp's face is white already and is left alone. **In the newsletter section** the presenting sponsors are a sentence of names until any has a logo, then "Presented by" and the sponsors side by side, each logo in the same room (132 by 40 pixels, in its own shape) with its size written on the tag, which Outlook needs (`emailMarks.ts`; the staff page measures each logo, `logoSizes`). Only PNG, JPEG and GIF count as a logo there; a sponsor whose logo is SVG or WebP is named instead. A saved picture's stamp draws any logo to fit.

**The envelope says there are prizes.** While there are prizes still to win, a line on the envelope ("Letters in the mailbag are entered in a drawing for six prizes.") counts every prize row and See the prizes takes the reader, and the keyboard's focus, down to the prizes, in a section of their own above the foot. Not on delivery day, when the winners lead the mailbag. **Each prize is a gift tag** (`PrizeList` in `Mailbag.tsx`): a pointed end with a punched hole, the envelope's stripes down its square end, the prize in the display face, who gives it under that with their logo beside it when they have one (for a prize donor this is where their name is seen), and "three to win" for a prize given more than once. It still reads as the plain line it was, "A $25 gift card from Rib River Pizza (three to win)": the parentheses are kept for a screen reader and a copied line and not drawn.

**Delivery day is a moment.** On Dec. 23 the envelope carries a rubber stamp, "Delivered · Dec. 23 · North Pole" (decoration: the line above it already says so), and the winners lead the mailbag under a large heading: each with a rosette, the line that names the prize, and the winning letter itself (`Winners` in `Mailbag.tsx`), side by side where there's room and centered when there are few. A letter is on the page once, in the first place that has it: the link a reader followed, then the winners, then the editor's pick, then the wall. So a winning letter isn't repeated on the wall, a pick that won is shown with the winners and not again as the pick, and a winner a reader followed a link to has its prize named beside the others without the letter shown twice.

**The stamps' place is held while the mailbag loads.** The loading envelope shows one blank stamp beside the title; with several presenting sponsors the row of stamps sits above the title on a phone, which would move the title down when it arrives. So the browser keeps the presenting sponsors' names from its last visit (`stampsKept.ts`: localStorage, names already printed on the page, wrapped as invariant 12 requires) and the loading envelope draws that many blank stamps and leaves room for their line. A first visit, a browser that refuses a frame storage, and the visit after a sponsor signs on still move once. With one sponsor nothing moves.

**What a sponsor's report can say.** Plausible counts `Sponsor Click` by sponsor and placement (a click on its banner ad is the `banner` placement), and `Letter Shared` (a picture saved or sent) and `Santa Reply Printed` carry the same `sponsor` property: whose stamp the picture or the printed sheet carried (`stampedBy` in `sales.ts`). Never which letter, and nothing before a sponsor signs on. The envelope, the strip and the thank-you page show every presenting sponsor, so their views are the pages' own views.

**The mailbag opens on a winter night** (`Night.tsx`, decided Nov. 15 from three mock-ups; the others were an evergreen "sorting desk" and a newspaper front page). The envelope lies a degree off square on a deep blue band the width of the page, with snow falling behind it, a moon, a horizon of pines and the North Pole's signpost, and snow at the foot that is the page's own color. Above the envelope is **the route**: the countdown's sentence, then a track from Wausau to the North Pole with the sleigh as far along it as the season is and the season's three dates under it (`route()` in `countdown.ts`). The phase decides which stretch the sleigh is on and the clock only places it within that stretch (invariant 1), so a device with the wrong date can't put it at the Pole early. The envelope carries an air-mail label and, on a wide screen, a postmark whose wavy lines reach the first stamp's edge and stop short of what's printed on it. **The editor's pick is a spread** on a wide screen: the child's own page taped down on the left, the typed words beside it; a phone keeps the photo above the words. The wall's letters lie a little off square and straighten when pointed at. All of it is drawn in the page (inline SVG and CSS), so nothing new is fetched; the scene is hidden from screen readers except the countdown; and the snow hangs still for a reader who asks for less motion. The loading envelope sits in the same scene with a bare track, so nothing moves when the season arrives. **The other reader pages share the night:** the form's page opens with a short band of it (`WriteNight` in `Write.tsx`: the heading and the countdown on a patch of the night's own color, the horizon at its foot), kept short so the first box to fill in is still on a phone's first screen, and the form's words and boxes stay on plain paper; the band is there while the form loads, with the countdown's line held open, so nothing under it moves when the season arrives; the thank-you page sets its small envelope on it, with the horizon under it; Santa's reply lies on it on screen (the sheet itself, and everything about how it prints, is unchanged: print styles take the blue, the snow, the moon and the horizon away); and the front-page strip is the same blue with its words in the moon's cream, without snow, since a flake standing still beside a line of words reads as an accent on a letter. Until the first letter is in, the strip's letters' place holds the countdown on its route, with the horizon along the band's foot and the moon in its corner, so it isn't a block of empty night for the days before letters open; once there are letters, they take that place. **The emails wear it as far as email allows** (`page()` in `_shared/letter_emails.ts`, for the confirmation, the posted note and the staff's digest): the envelope's airmail edge as a row of colored table cells, a band of the night's blue with the title in the moon's cream, then the words on the letter's paper. Tables, cell colors and fonts every program has (Georgia for the display face); no picture to block, and no striped or gradient background, which Outlook doesn't draw. The head says only the feature's name and WPR's, so the confirmation still carries nothing typed into the form (invariant 11). **Each email has a line for the inbox** (`preview()`: the words a mail program shows beside the subject, hidden once the email is open; without one it showed the band's title a second time): the confirmation's says what happens next and, like the rest of it, nothing typed into the form; the posted note's names the child; the digest's names the first three children waiting, or the form's trouble. **The mailbag before its first letter says how it works** (`HowItWorks` in `Mailbag.tsx`): three steps where the letters will be (a grown-up sends it, an editor reads it, Santa gets it), each number in a postmark's ring, side by side on a wide screen on a dashed line that ends in the route's star. It says only what the site does: the reply to print goes with the email a family gets when the letter is up, and the drawing is named only once there are prizes, as on the envelope.

The repo is `wpr-santa-letters`; it must match `base` in `web/vite.config.ts` (`/wpr-santa-letters/`).

**Live in production (as of Nov. 10, 2026):** Supabase project `vhimfuaproejbxahjgpr` (WPR's Pro organization), the app at https://rowanflynnpilot.github.io/wpr-santa-letters/. Every change goes on a branch and through a PR; never push straight to `main`, since a push to `main` deploys (migrations, functions and the web app).

## Invariants — keep these true

1. **The calendar is the state machine.** `phase_at(season, ts)` is the only phase logic: `upcoming` → `open` (the form takes letters) → `closed` (staff finish up and draw) → `delivered`. The web app reads `phase` from `current_season`; nothing is toggled by hand. The envelope's countdown (`countdown.ts`: "Five days left to send a letter", then days to delivery) takes that phase as given and only counts Wausau calendar days to the date it's waiting for; if the device's clock disagrees with the phase, it says nothing.
2. **Parents send, children don't.** The form is filled in by a parent or legal guardian on the child's behalf, with their consent, which keeps the form clear of COPPA's rules on collecting information directly from children under 13. Never add a field that asks a child for anything, or that publishes more than a first name, age and town.
3. **Public vs. private data.** `letters` holds only what is published: first name, age (0–17; 0 shows as "under 1"), town (optional), the letter, an optional photo. Who sent it, the parent's name and email, a mailed letter's return address, consent, the newsletter opt-in, eligibility and the review trail live in `letter_private` (staff only). No direct writes to either: RPCs only.
4. **One set of rules for the words.** `letter_words()` cleans and checks the first name, age, town and letter for the form (`submit_letter`), staff entry (`add_letter`) and staff edits (`edit_letter`), so the three can't drift. Names and towns are one line (`one_line`), so a last name can't ride along on a second line, and an invisible control character pasted into one is dropped rather than refused. Messages are plain English (`P0001`) and the form shows them as they are; any other error is logged and the parent sees a retry message.
5. **Form letters wait; staff letters don't.** `submit_letter` (service role, from the function only) saves a letter as `pending`; `add_letter` (staff) saves it `approved`, since the staff member typing it is the reviewer. Staff keep the child's spelling and take out last names, street addresses and schools. **A page that's out of date can't undo another editor:** `moderate_letter` takes the status the page showed and `edit_letter` the last edit it saw, and each refuses if the letter has changed since, so a stale tab can't post a letter someone just declined or put back a last name someone took out. **A photo that shows too much, and the photo of any letter that isn't posted or comes down, is deleted:** `remove_letter_photo`, `moderate_letter` (declining or taking down) and `decline_letters` clear `photo_id` and queue it in `photo_deletions`, in the same transaction; the staff browser then deletes both files and the queue row. If that answer never reaches the browser (the tab closed, the network dropped), the next time Review letters opens it finishes the queue; if a delete fails, the staff page says so with the folder to delete by hand. Photos are cached for an hour, not a year: on Supabase Pro the Smart CDN drops a deleted photo within about a minute, but a browser that already loaded it keeps its copy for as long as the cache header says. **Junk is declined in bulk:** on Waiting for review, Find narrows the list, Select all shown (or a letter's own Select) picks them, and `decline_letters` declines them all with one reason, refusing if any has changed since the page loaded.
6. **Explicit privileges only.** Migration 0003 revokes everything from `anon`, `authenticated` and `service_role` and grants exactly what's needed; readers see a winner's draw but not who ran it. Postgres still lets PUBLIC execute any new function until it's revoked, which no per-schema default can change, so a migration that adds a function revokes it from public itself (as 0005 does). `04_privileges.test.sql` lists every table privilege and function each API role has, so anything new that grants access, on purpose or not, fails CI until the list is updated. The suite passes with and without Supabase's permissive default grants.
7. **Security definer functions** set `search_path = ''` and fully qualify names. **Views** are `security_invoker`. `04_privileges` checks both, and that every table has RLS on.
8. **Photos:** the browser decodes once as an `<img>`, which applies the photo's orientation in every browser in use (Safari since 13.1; `createImageBitmap`'s option for it needs Safari 17), lays it on white (a transparent PNG drawing stays readable), and encodes a 1600px full image and a 640px thumbnail. `submit-letter` strips APP1/APP13 (Exif/XMP/IPTC, GPS included) from both, stores them as `letter-photos/<photo_id>/full.jpg` and `/thumb.jpg` with an hour's cache header (invariant 5), then calls `submit_letter()`; if the letter isn't saved, it removes both. Staff uploads (a scan of a mailed letter) go through the same browser encoding, which writes no metadata; `add_letter` refuses a photo whose two files aren't both uploaded. The only `storage.objects` policies let staff insert, see and delete in `letter-photos`. **In the mailbag the photo leads its letter** (`LetterCard`): the child's own hand first, the typed words under it. The wall loads the 640px thumbnail, in a frame no taller than 4:5 that's held open before the photo arrives; a taller page shows its top, with a button that opens all of it from the full-size file. Only the one letter a reader followed a link to loads the full-size file up front.
9. **Bots stop at the door.** `submit-letter` verifies the Turnstile token with Cloudflare before it stores anything. One email address can send at most 10 letters in a rolling day, its +tags counted together, under the season's lock so parallel sends can't slip past. **A retry never makes a second letter:** the form gives each letter a key (`submission_key`, kept across retries, new for the next letter); if a try saved the letter but its reply was lost, the retry gets that letter back from `submit_letter` (`repeat: true`), the function deletes the retry's copy of the photo, and the emails go again with the first try's idempotency keys, which Resend drops.
10. **The drawing is fair.** `run_prize_drawing` runs after letters close, only when no letter is waiting for review, and draws each prize once; run again, it draws only prizes added since (a late sponsor) and never touches a winner. Every letter in the mailbag is a ticket, except staff households' and letters with no way to reach the family; one prize per family. A staff household is a family: `set_prize_eligible` changes every letter of the family, and a letter that arrives later takes the family's mark (`keep_household`). `family_key` is the email without any +tag, or the return address reduced to lowercase letters and digits ("12 Elm St., Wausau" = "12 elm st wausau"); a mailed letter and a form letter from one family only match if staff enter the parent's email on the mailed one. `redraw_prize(draw_id)` forfeits that exact draw (a page that's out of date can't forfeit a newer winner) and draws again among families holding nothing. A current winner can't be taken down or its family marked a staff household. Once the drawing has run, no ticket is added: no new letter, no declined letter posted, no household put back. **The season lock** (`lock_season`, an advisory lock taken first by the drawing, a redraw, a moderation, an eligibility change, a new staff letter and a new form letter) keeps two editors from interleaving: two redraws at once can't hand one family two prizes. Winners are public from `delivered_at`, current winners only. "One family" is matched by address, not by name: two parents' different emails, or a mailed letter typed in without the parent's email, count as two families, so glance down the winners for a shared last name or street before contacting them (decided Nov. 7: that check is enough). Sponsors' households can enter and win; only staff households can't.
11. **Fail fast.** Missing env vars stop the function at load (`SUPABASE_SECRET_KEYS` needs a `default` key) and the web build at build time. Two settings are optional: without `RESEND_API_KEY`, letters save and nothing is sent; without `ANTHROPIC_API_KEY`, Add a letter can't read a photo (invariant 24). Anyone can type any address into the form, so the parent's confirmation carries nothing typed into it (no names, no town, no words): the form can't be used to send someone's chosen text from santa@ to a stranger. The editor's morning digest (invariant 20) carries a short excerpt of each waiting letter, to staff only; the posted email, which goes only after an editor has read the letter, names the child. The function logs a database error's code and message only, never its details, which repeat the family's words.
12. **The mailbag and the form never load the Supabase client.** Inside WordPress the app is a third-party frame, and a browser that blocks third-party cookies (Chrome Incognito, by default) refuses storage there; the client opens storage as it loads, which took the form down until Nov. 7. `reads.ts` and `send.ts` use plain fetch with the publishable key, in the `apikey` header only (it isn't a JWT; `Authorization` carries a signed-in staff member's token or nothing). 13. **The posted email goes once, and only for a posted letter.** When staff post a letter that has an email (Post it, or Add a letter with an email), the staff browser calls `letter-posted`, which checks the caller is staff, sends the parent a note with a link to the letter and one to Santa's reply (invariant 22), then stamps `posted_notice_at`. `posted_notice()` answers only for an approved letter with an email and no stamp, and Resend's idempotency key (`santa-letters-<id>-posted`) drops a repeat, so a double click or a retry can't send twice. It names the child and never carries the letter; whether it mentions the drawing and invites more letters comes from `posted_notice()` (the family isn't a staff household; `phase_at` says open), so the email has no phase logic of its own.

At a computer the card also offers Facebook with the same link (`Letter Shared`, method `facebook`). A letter's controls are small pills with a glyph each (Share, Facebook, Save picture; `.chip`), 32px to the eye and 44px to a thumb. Its tests are `web/src/shareWorker_test.ts`. `embed.js` turns it into a fresh frame at `?letter=<id>&from=…#/mailbag`, a new iframe rather than a new `src`, because Chrome can let a frame's first load win over a `src` changed while it's under way. `linked.ts` reads it once, first, and takes it off the address before analytics loads: Plausible records where the reader came from (`Letter Opened`, `from`) and never which letter. The mailbag shows that letter first and scrolls to it. Santa's reply is the one link straight to the app, `?letter=<id>&from=<email|staff>#/reply`, read the same way (`Santa Reply Opened`, `Santa Reply Printed`).

## Operations (SQL editor)

Sponsors, their logos and their prizes are added on the staff page now (Sponsors and prizes, invariant 29). The SQL for them below still works and is the way around the page if it's ever needed.

From a Windows `psql`, set `PGCLIENTENCODING=UTF8` first, or curly apostrophes in sponsor names garble.

```sql
-- Staff: each user must exist first (Dashboard > Authentication > Add user). Shereen signs in with editor@.
insert into public.staff (user_id) select id from auth.users
where email in ('editor@wausaupilotandreview.com', 'rowan.flynn@wausaupilotandreview.com');

-- A presenting sponsor (four a season at most; a fifth is refused): logo_path is the logo's object name in
-- the sponsor-logos bucket, uploaded first; leave it out until the logo arrives, and the stamp sets the name
-- in type. Names up to 80 characters. Their stamps sit in the order the rows were added.
insert into public.sponsors (season_id, name, tier, logo_path, website_url)
select id, 'Example Bank', 'presenting', 'example-bank.png', 'https://example.com' from public.seasons where year = 2026;

-- A supporting sponsor.
insert into public.sponsors (season_id, name, tier, website_url)
select id, 'Example Pizza', 'supporting', 'https://example.com' from public.seasons where year = 2026;

-- A prize donor (a prize, no fee): named with its prize and at the foot, not in the row under the envelope.
-- Its prize is a row like any other (below). A logo is optional: it shows at the foot.
insert into public.sponsors (season_id, name, tier, website_url)
select id, 'Example Candy', 'donor', 'https://example.com' from public.seasons where year = 2026;

-- Prizes: one row per prize to win, written to follow "wins" (lowercase "a"; the database refuses "A …",
-- "An …" and "The …"), up to 120 characters.
-- A sponsor giving three gift cards gets three rows; the mailbag lists them once, "(three to win)".
-- The drawing gives them out in the order they were added.
insert into public.prizes (season_id, sponsor_id, description)
select s.id, sp.id, 'a family pizza night'
from public.seasons s join public.sponsors sp on sp.season_id = s.id and sp.name = 'Example Pizza'
where s.year = 2026;

-- A logo that arrives later.
update public.sponsors set logo_path = 'example-pizza.png' where name = 'Example Pizza';

-- Newsletter opt-ins from the form, for import: posted letters only, so a junk letter's address never lands on the list.
-- Export them before Jan. 6: the clearing (invariant 19) takes the addresses with everything else.
select distinct p.email from public.letter_private p join public.letters l on l.id = p.letter_id
where p.newsletter_opt_in and l.status = 'approved' order by p.email;

-- The two daily jobs (clearing families' details, the morning digest), and how their runs went. A digest
-- run "succeeded" once pg_net queued the call; the call's own answer is in net._http_response.
select jobname, schedule, command from cron.job;
select j.jobname, d.status, d.return_message, d.start_time
from cron.job_run_details d join cron.job j using (jobid) order by d.start_time desc limit 10;
select status_code, content, created from net._http_response order by created desc limit 5;

-- Send this morning's digest now (it sends once a day; a second call that day is dropped by Resend).
-- Paste the command from cron.job where jobname = 'santa-letters-staff-digest'.

-- Replacing a Vault secret (a rotated secret key, a new project URL): not here. Use the dashboard's Vault
-- screen (Integrations > Vault > Secrets) and edit `santa_secret_key` or `santa_project_url` in place,
-- keeping the name. Production's were set there, not with vault.create_secret from the SQL editor.
-- Check the digest's job still reads them (both rows, no values shown):
select name, updated_at from vault.secrets where name in ('santa_project_url', 'santa_secret_key');

-- Moving a date (here, opening a week earlier).
update public.seasons set entries_open_at = timestamptz '2026-11-20 00:00 America/Chicago' where year = 2026;

-- Photos left in storage with no letter (a form upload that failed partway, or a queued deletion no one
-- has opened Review to finish): their folders, to delete in Storage > letter-photos. A form letter's
-- folder exists a moment before its letter does, so ignore any from the last few minutes.
select split_part(name, '/', 1) as folder, min(created_at) as uploaded
from storage.objects
where bucket_id = 'letter-photos'
  and split_part(name, '/', 1) not in (select photo_id::text from public.letters where photo_id is not null)
group by 1 order by 2;
```
